The artist has little leverage, so the real question is whether any deal on the table beats staying independent.

Instructor notes

Setup

  1. Students open the game’s web link in any browser. They don’t need accounts.
  2. Open the class dashboard on your computer and project or share the five-letter class code.
  3. Pair teams of 2–4, one device per team. One team starts a game (entering the class code) and reads its four-letter game code to the other team.
  4. Every pair negotiates the same artist, so results are directly comparable. For a second round, have teams switch sides.
  5. If the internet is down, use one-screen mode: teams pass a laptop between turns.

Rules of play

  • 480 Collective makes the opening offer. The teams then alternate offers, and each offer can include a short note.
  • Either side can accept the other side’s offer on its turn, or walk away at any time.
  • After the artist team’s sixth offer, the label’s response is final: the artist must accept or walk.
  • Each team sees only its own value meter and walk-away point. Briefs are confidential — no showing screens.

Debrief

  • Open the class dashboard and compare deals for the same client. Why did outcomes differ so much?
  • Which terms were cheap for the label to give but valuable to the artist? Did teams find those trades?
  • Who used the performance-based terms (advance escalator, option bump, tour shelter)? Why will a label give those more easily than cash up front?
  • Use the “signing vs. staying independent” table on the results screen. In which outcomes does signing clearly win, and what did Hollis give up to get it?
  • Did any artist team sign a deal that scored below zero? Why is walking away so hard when it’s the only leverage you have?
  • Did anyone bluff about a competing offer? How did the label team decide whether to believe it?
  • Find a deal where the artist is unrecouped in the “expected” case while the label is profitable. Walk the class through why.
  • Did any pair fail to close even though a deal was possible? What got in the way?

Scoring

Each team’s score is the share of the available surplus it captured: how far above its own walk-away point the final deal landed, compared with the best deal the other side would have signed. Walking away scores 0. For the artist team, the walk-away point is the estimated value of staying independent, so signing a deal below it scores negative and walking away would have been the better result. The numbers are illustrative, based on a simplified model of royalties, recoupment, 360 income, option albums and catalog value across flop, expected and breakout outcomes. The label weighs the flop case more heavily than the artist does, which is why terms that only pay out on success are cheaper for it to give.
